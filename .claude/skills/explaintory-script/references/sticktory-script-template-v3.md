DRAMATIC ENGINE  (the SOUL — read before the skeleton)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
Structure alone makes a documentary tour of an institution. That is NOT what the outliers are.
They are personal STORIES with characters, choices, and betrayals; the history is just the stage.
A version with full structure + voice + pacing but no dramatic engine reads ~75% right and still
falls flat. Three rules turn the tour into a tragedy:

RULE 1 — AGENCY (a want).
  "You" must WANT something and make active choices — reach for a goal, defy something, protect
  someone — never just passively endure. The fall only lands because you were reaching for something.
  A passive hero who only absorbs what happens has WEATHER, not a tragedy.
  → Give "you" a person to love and possibly lose, and one thing you're quietly fighting to keep.

RULE 2 — A VILLAIN WITH A FACE.
  A specific PERSON holds the knife — a rival, a brother, family, a friend who turns — not a faceless
  institution. A system can grind you down, but it can't BETRAY you, and the best endings run on betrayal.
  The antagonist needs a "who," not just a "what." (Pharaoh's brothers assassinate him. Concubine's
  rivals poison her. Spartan: the shield-brother is turned and steps back, letting you die.)

RULE 3 — THE "BIG MISTAKE."
  The reversal/death is caused by YOUR OWN trust or misjudgment, not just bad luck. The audience watches
  a person walk into their own ending because they believed the wrong thing. Use the literal beat —
  "you assumed your brothers wouldn't still want you dead. Big mistake."
  → It lands ONLY at the reversal (see DON'T-TELEGRAPH). Never pre-announce it.

CRAFT 4 — LET PAYOFFS BREATHE.
  At the big emotional beat (betrayal, death, reversal) SLOW DOWN and linger a beat before moving on.
  Don't rush past the feeling. Outliers stack short beats + one sensory/emotional detail at the payoff
  (Aztec lingers on the view from the pyramid — "Held down. Trapped. Waiting." — before the knife).
  "Moving on before the emotion lands" is the #1 recurring failure. This is the one place the velocity
  rule below yields: no dead air everywhere EXCEPT the payoff, which gets its breath.

CRAFT 5 — DON'T TELEGRAPH THE TWIST.
  Never step out as narrator to pre-explain the mistake ("That's your mistake, by the way…"). Bury the
  setup in action; let "you" SINCERELY believe the bond/plan so the betrayal genuinely surprises.
  Soft foreshadow tics ("hopefully that's not foreshadowing," "Spoiler: the machine usually wins") are
  fine — they tease without spoiling. Explaining the trick in advance is not.

VOICE RULES  (non-negotiable)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
PERSPECTIVE:  2nd person, present tense. Reader IS the character. Narrator may wink in 1st person:
              "And when I say don't make it, I mean they literally die." / "Trust me, you'll need it."
RHYTHM:       Short-short-FRAGMENT-longer. Punch with 1–3 word fragments: "Meat, forget it." "Wait, what?"
              "Nope." "Big mistake." "No pressure, right?" Then one flowing sentence, then snap back.
              TARGETS: avg ~8.5 w/sentence · ~28% fragments (≤4w; 22–35% range) · ~59% short (≤8w) · ~5% long (≥20w; never 3 longs in a row).
              EXCEPTION: at the emotional payoff, slow down and let it breathe (Craft 4). Velocity everywhere else.
READING LEVEL: ~6th–8th grade. Conversational, never academic.
OPINION DENSITY: A sarcastic aside every 2–3 sentences. The dark-comedy narrator IS the product.
ANACHRONISM ENGINE: Constantly compare the period to modern life — "hit the jackpot," "level up,"
              "game over," "9-to-5," "K-pop trainee," "imperial sugar daddy starter pack,"
              "Pharaoh Amazon Prime," "Egyptian Barbie," "Viking Christmas," "Spotify playlist."

HUMOR ENGINE  (the laugh-per-breath that the structure won't give you)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════
DENSITY: ~2.3 humor signals per 100 words. Densest in the INSTITUTIONAL / setup beats (the cradle-to-grave
  tour is your comedy playground). The dramatic climax stays mostly STRAIGHT, then lands cold.
  NOTE: keyword "humor scorers" are noisy and undercount novel phrasings — judge by reading ALOUD, not a number.

THE FIVE LEVERS:
  1. ANACHRONISM / MODERN COMPARISON — the main engine (see Voice Rules). Most of the laughs live here.
  2. SARCASTIC NARRATOR ASIDE — "Great role model. Very healthy lesson for the children." "Cute." "Romantic."
  3. DARK-COMIC UNDERCUT — grim fact, then a joke a line later. BODY ONLY, never the final line.
     ("At least your sacrifice means good crops." "boys die at this. Over cheese." "You call it Tuesday.")
  4. THE REACTING NARRATOR — the BIGGEST lever and the easiest to miss. The narrator reacts out loud to the
     absurdity alongside the viewer: "Are you serious? This is how you die?" "guess what" "imagine"
     "good luck with that" "Surprise." Outliers do this ~6×/script; weak drafts do it ~1×. Lean on it.
  5. CASUAL WORDPLAY / TICS — keep the loose spoken voice EVEN in dark/dramatic stretches (drama must not
     drift earnest or literary). "basically" is a genuine tic (~20× across outliers — the single most common filler).

RECURRING TICS (use 3–5 per video):
  - Cold open in MODE A or MODE B          - Sarcastic "Congrats" / "you hit the jackpot"
  - Ironic foreshadow: "spoiler alert" / "you jinxed it" / "hopefully that's not foreshadowing"
  - "And guess what?" / "Right? … Right."  - "And just like that, …"   - "But hey, …"   - "basically …"
  - Age/year transitions: "By {age}…", "At {age}…", "Fast forward, you're {age}."
  - Cold death/cycle closing line

NEVER USE (absent from every script — read as AI or as a different channel):
  ✗ "Furthermore," "Moreover," "In conclusion," "It's important to note," "Let's dive in"
  ✗ "Delve," "tapestry," "nestled," "stands as a testament," "weave"
  ✗ "Little did they know" (use "spoiler alert")     ✗ "Have you ever wondered" / "we" / "us"
  ✗ "Historians believe," "records suggest" — no hedging, just assert it
  ✗ Mid-video "smash like and subscribe" — the ONLY allowed interruption is a native in-world sponsor
  ✗ TELEGRAPHING the twist — never "That's your mistake, by the way…" or stepping out to pre-explain the betrayal.
    The protagonist believes sincerely; "Big mistake" lands only AT the reversal.
  ✗ Undercutting the FINAL line with a joke — the body undercuts, the gut-punch lands cold.
  ✗ Drama beats drifting earnest/literary — keep the casual voice running through the dark stretches.
