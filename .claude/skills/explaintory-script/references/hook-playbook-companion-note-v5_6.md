# Companion note — Two opener shapes (sync with channel skill v5.6)

> Append this to `hook-structure-playbook.md`. It reconciles the playbook's one rule with the second opener shape added to the channel skill in v5.6. Nothing here overrides the playbook; it names a variant the playbook already half-allows.

## The one rule still holds — with two ways to satisfy it

The playbook's rule is: **sentence one names the subject and negates an assumption about it.** That remains the spine. v5.6 just makes explicit that there are two *moves* that satisfy it, and you should pick per subject instead of defaulting to one.

**Shape A — Straight reveal (the playbook's default).**
Name the subject accurately in sentence one, negate the assumption in the same breath. The proven outlier openers are this shape:
- "The earliest weapon wasn't designed at all." (*not X*)
- "Rome had no navy, so it invented a way to fight without one." (*had no X, so Y*)
- "Its weapon was not armor or artillery, it was speed." (*not X — Y*)

Use Shape A when the subject is famous, or when the truth itself is the hook.

**Shape B — Misdirect-then-deflate.**
Lead with what the subject *looks like* (a vivid wrong impression or a nickname), hold the precise identity one beat, then name it as the correction. The wrong first impression is the negated assumption; the real name is the payoff.
- "The meat grinder. A giant spinning wheel the size of a room, racing toward you on the rails. It is a snowplow."
- "No, that is not a science-fiction spaceship. It was a real aircraft from the 1950s."

Use Shape B when the subject has a striking but misleading appearance (most weird/visual hardware).

## How Shape B stays inside the playbook's rules

Shape B looks like it breaks "name the subject in sentence one," but it doesn't, because the playbook already says: **"Anchor to something the viewer already knows first, name the obscure stuff after."** In Shape B, the *nickname or visual stand-in* is the anchor that opens the line ("the meat grinder," "a flying donut"), and it still names what the thing *is* loosely; only the precise proper noun is withheld. That is anchoring-familiar-first, not name-burying.

So Shape B is legal precisely when it obeys the playbook's two existing don'ts:
- **Don't front-load unknown proper nouns.** A nickname or a plain visual ("a giant spinning wheel") is a familiar anchor. "The Schienenzeppelin was…" as sentence one is not — that's the flop pattern.
- **Don't go spec-first with no frame.** The misdirect must *be* the frame. Lead with the impossibility/impression; let the name and number confirm it.

## The one hard caveat (the failure mode to grep for)

Shape B only works when the **deflation is surprising** — a genuine gap between impression and reality (terrifying machine → just a snowplow; spaceship → 1950s aircraft). If you misdirect and the reveal is exactly as dramatic as the setup, you've only delayed information for no payoff. That is the playbook's existing "spec-first with no frame" failure wearing a costume. When the impression and the reality are equally dramatic, or when the subject is famous, fall back to Shape A.

## Build-checklist add-on

Add to the playbook's per-section build checklist:
- **0. Pick the opener shape.** Famous subject or truth-is-the-hook → Shape A. Striking-but-misleading appearance → Shape B. If Shape B, confirm the deflation is genuinely surprising before committing; if it isn't, switch to Shape A.
