# Scriptwriter Brief - Horror Explainer Channel

Read this once, keep it open while you write. It's the house style in plain form. Follow it and your drafts pass QC with almost no notes. The full system exists behind this, but everything that actually gets flagged is on this page.

## The format in one look

- Second-person hooks. Every creature opens with the viewer in a scene: "You're driving...", "You hear...", "You notice...". Then the reveal line: "That is [name]."
- Name as the opener. Each section starts with the creature's name on its own, no preamble.
- Hard cut between creatures. No transition sentences, no story that carries across creatures. Each one is its own module.
- Shape per creature (default six beats): hook (viewer in a scene) - reveal ("That is X") - what it looks like with a size - how it behaves / how it kills - one lore or unknown beat - one dry closing line. This is the default. Your assignment sheet may tell you to adapt it for the title (fewer beats, a different closer, an origin block instead of survival). When it does, follow the sheet, not this default.
- Length: about 180 to 230 words per creature. Write the section, don't pad to a number. Let sections vary.
- Total for an 8-creature video: about 1,700 words, roughly 9 to 10 minutes.

## The ten rules that get flagged most (memorize these)

1. Invent nothing. Never make up a height, weight, date, incident, or victim. Weights almost never have a real source, so leave them out unless the sheet gives you one. Use only the numbers on your assignment sheet.
2. When a number is contested, say so instead of picking one. "Estimates vary wildly, from around two stories to nearly six." A hedged range can't be wrong in the comments. A made-up exact number can.
3. Every creature must match the title. On a "Largest" video, every creature reads big. Carry scale through the description even when there's no measured height ("a towering, elongated figure that stands well above head height").
4. Descriptions must be internally consistent and match the name. A creature called Housewalker walks. Don't also say it flies. The editor draws exactly what you write, so a contradictory line becomes a broken shot.
5. Never open two creatures the same way, especially back to back. Rotate the setting, the character, and the first wrong detail. If two creatures both want a road scene, re-cast one.
6. No two sentences in a row without something you can show on screen. Abstract ideas ("sun symbols mean different things in different cultures") are dead air. Give the editor a picture ("the afternoon going heavy, like the sun is leaning on your shoulders").
7. Confident voice. State the unknown without apologizing. Not "very little is confirmed" but "no one has ever gotten a clean look at its face, and it seems to like it that way."
8. One creator name-drop per section, max. Spend it on the strongest piece of canon (a real quote, the killer caption). Phrase everything else in-world.
9. One dry joke per section, max, and only in the closing line. Never during an attack or death beat. Deadpan, not slang. If in doubt, use a creepy closer instead.
10. No em dashes, anywhere. Use commas or periods. This one is easy and it matters for the workflow.

## Banned words

terrifying, horrifying, nightmarish, disturbing, creepy, entity. Use no more than once each in a whole script. Fear comes from concrete detail, not adjectives.

## Banned AI-writing tells (this is what QC hunts for)

- Fragment ladders: "Not like an animal. Like a drawing." Cut these. One short impact sentence per section is fine if it carries information ("Then it moved.").
- Rhetorical filler: "Here's the thing", "But here's where it gets worse", "Little did they know".
- Emotion labels: "a chill ran down his spine", "her hands went cold". Show the detail that causes the feeling instead.
- Telling the viewer how to feel: "that should scare you". Just show it.

## Two CTAs, woven in, unlabeled

After creature 3, a short like-and-subscribe line that flows as part of the script (no "CTA" label). At the very end, a one-line comment prompt plus a "stay safe" sign-off. Keep them in the flow, not marked as separate blocks.

## What you do NOT decide

Roster, creature order, universe, and the canon numbers come to you on an assignment sheet. Don't add or drop creatures, don't reorder, don't source your own facts. If a fact you need isn't on the sheet, flag it and ask. Your job is the writing; the research and roster are locked before you start.
