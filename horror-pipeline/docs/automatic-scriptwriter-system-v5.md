## PART 1 — Style DNA: What M Simplified Actually Does

Evidence from 7 full transcripts: Biggest Doctor Nowhere (1.9M views), Trevor Giants (1.5M), Japanese Legends Pt.2 (887K), Creepiest SCPs (593K), Sea Creatures (583K), Analog Horror Largest (472K), Largest SCPs (472K).

**Creature counts are consistent: 6–8 per video** (6 for the SCP video with longer named-victim stories, 7 for sea creatures, 8 everywhere else). Total words 1,514–1,871.

**The formula per creature (verified in every section):**

1. **Name as its own opener.** "Amber." / "House Walker." / "SCP-096, the Shy Guy." No preamble, ever. The first video word of the whole video is the first creature's name.
2. **Dramatized third-person encounter (measured: 120–140 words).** An ordinary person in an ordinary place: "One night in a quiet town, a young man noticed his neighbor's lights flicker." (Measured from transcripts: Amber 122, Sun Man 127, Watch Tower 132, SCP-096 142 — the freelancer brief's "70–100 words" guess undershot reality.) Escalation is always: *normal → one small wrong detail → closer look → reveal → attack, escape, or disappearance.* The creature is never fully shown in sentence one.
3. **Explanation block (~60–90 words).** Pivot sentence is always a flat definition: "Amber is a tall, distorted humanoid with a reddish-brown body and extremely long limbs estimated to stand around 23 m in height." Covers body, size (metric + feet for big numbers), signature feature, movement.
4. **Survival block (~20–35 words).** Verbatim formula: "To survive this creature, …" followed by 2–3 short imperatives. "Stay away from windows at night… If seen, do not look at it and create distance immediately."
5. **Hard cut to next creature's name.** Zero transition sentences.

**Formula variants confirmed across all 7 videos:**
- **Folklore/urban-legend variant** (Japanese legends, sea creatures): survival block replaced by an **origin block**: "Nureonna is a traditional Japanese yokai from Edo period folklore… Her name means wet woman." Story → appearance → history/meaning. Constant hedges: "According to the legend…", "In many versions…", "Some accounts say…"
- **SCP variant** (Largest SCPs): named victims ("Jason woke up at 5:00 a.m. excited for his first fishing trip with his uncle.") and a recurring pivot line: "That was when the Foundation realized the horrifying truth." Then "To survive this entity, never…"
- **Analog horror variant** (Analog Horror Largest): same story→definition→survival skeleton, but stories lean on dated series lore (the 1995 Vita Carnis facility fire, the 1985 explorers, the 2021 Ever Given) and survival lines get an ominous tag: "…because once a Special Tree chooses you, reality itself may never let you return."
- **Encyclopedia hybrid** (Trevor Giants): some sections skip the story entirely and go full nature-documentary ("Sky Mantas breed about every 5 years… gestation lasts around 3 years"). No "To survive" lines in that video at all; definitions name the creator explicitly ("…created by Trevor Henderson").

**Signature sentence rhythm:**
- 8–16 word sentences, punctuated by 2–4 word impact sentences: "Then it moved." / "He froze." / "That is the trap." / "No sound, no struggle." / "It was looking straight at him."
- Escalation connectors: "At first… Then… The moment he… Within seconds…"
- Paired negations for dread: "It doesn't roll. It doesn't slide. It walks." / "It didn't run. It didn't chase. It just got closer, and the smile never changed."

**CTAs (near-verbatim, consistent across videos):**
- Mid-roll, after creature 3 (~3-minute mark): "If you've made it this far, you probably like this kind of content. So, don't forget to like, subscribe, and drop a comment. It helps the video reach more people and supports the channel."
- Outro: "If you enjoyed this video, make sure to like and subscribe. It really motivates me to create more interesting and scary content for you. Also, tell me in the comments which creature or horror topic you want to see in the next video. Stay safe, and I'll see you in the next one."

**Humor:** almost none. Maybe one dry line per video ("Don't trust the smile."). Fear comes from detail, not adjectives.

**Numbers:** M Simplified states exact heights/weights confidently ("around 23 m", "36,000 to 72,000 kg"). Many of these are fan estimates. **We keep the confident delivery but source-check first** — see Part 5.

**Video math:** 8 creatures + 2 CTAs ≈ 1,650–1,760 words ≈ 8:40–9:30 runtime (~190 wpm narration). Budget ~185–205 words per creature.

## PART 2 — Style DNA: What Ficknime Actually Does

Evidence from 7 full transcripts: Doctor Nowhere 16-min (454K views), Every Doctor Nowhere Monster (407K), Analog Horror Largest (380K), Kindest SCPs (131K), Analog Giants Pt.2 (110K), Apollyon SCPs (95K), Doctor Nowhere Largest (69K).

**Creature counts vary by format — this is the big difference from M Simplified:** 10–12 creatures for monster-list videos (~150–185 words each), and **21 creatures for SCP compilation videos** (~120–140 words each, no stories, pure explanation). Ficknime compresses; M Simplified dramatizes. Word totals 1,573–2,973.

**The newer, better-performing formula (per creature):**

1. **Name, then a second-person hook (~20–35 words).** "You are driving home late at night when you notice a giant water tower standing in the distance. Nothing unusual — until you realize there is a massive eye staring directly at the city."
2. **The reveal line:** "**That is the Watchtower.**" — a fixed catchphrase pivot used in almost every section.
3. **Explanation (~80–120 words).** What it looks like, size, where it comes from (names the series: Gemini Home Entertainment, Vita Carnis, Local 58), and **one strong dated incident** told fast: "In 1991, a space probe called Crusader 5 finally got close enough to investigate. What it found was terrifying."
4. **Punchline closer — every single section ends with a dry twist:**
   - "So, if you see a deer skull moving through the woods, do not worry about what is in front of you. Worry about how long it has been feeding."
   - "So, yeah, if you ever find a weird tree, maybe leave it alone."
   - "So, if this oversized meat stick ever wakes up again, you can go ask questions. I am leaving."

**The older Ficknime formula** (Every Doctor Nowhere Monster, 407K) is wiki-style casual with heavy internet slang: "Bro really said normal wasn't an option." / "like your brain just exposed you in 4K." It's funny but dates fast, and Ficknime themselves moved away from it in newer videos. **We take the dry wit, we leave the "bro/rizz" slang** — this matches the owner's brief.

**Other Ficknime traits:** no CTAs at all — in any of the 7 videos; more lore-history and series context than M Simplified; comfortable saying "nobody actually knows" and making the mystery itself the hook ("A giant smiling creature that appeared only once and left behind nothing but questions.").

**Additional confirmed FK patterns from the wider sample:**
- **Hook rotation:** "You are…" scenes, "Imagine…", "What if I told you…", "Ever had a nightmare that felt so real…?", and joke premises ("Most monsters want to eat people. Father Fester apparently looked at that idea and said, 'Nah, I will eat everything.'"). The "That is the ___" reveal is standard in analog horror videos, optional elsewhere.
- **SCP compilation mode:** cold open on entry 1, cold end on the last entry, no outro. Sections are compact wiki-style: "SCP-XXXX is a…" → mechanics → ominous or funny closer. Hook-question openers sprinkled in: "SCP-6000. Half of Earth is already gone, and nobody died."
- **Tone breaks:** deliberately harmless/funny entries placed mid-list as palate cleansers (parmesan-seeking SCP, Soap, Windmill Girl) — pacing tool, not filler.
- **Punchline closers double as fake survival tips:** "So, if it ever approaches you, pretend you are late for something and disappear." / "So, if a giant puppet starts rolling toward you, do not waste time wondering who is controlling it. Just run."

## PART 3 — The House Voice (the merged style we write in)

**THE CREATIVE LICENSE RULE — THE SYSTEM IS THE BASE, NOT A CAGE (governing principle, owner decision 2026-07-17).** This rule sits above every style rule below and every beat structure in Part 4. The competitor transcripts prove it: no strong section they publish reads like a checklist was followed — each one has a moment where a human writer clearly made a choice the formula didn't dictate (the Boiled One's "just paralyze" beat, Ficknime's "you live in a zoo now"). That choice-making is the product. Concretely:

- **Per-section adaptability:** write each section the way its best version wants to be written. Sections in one video do not have to share a rhythm — one can be a slow single scene, the next a fast jokey escalation. The question to ask per section is "what would a professional storyteller in this niche do to make THIS one hit?" — then do that, not the template.
- **Beats are defaults, not laws:** reorder, merge, or drop beats when the material calls for it (a killer caption can BE the whole reveal; a definition pivot can arrive late for effect). When a structural rule is broken on purpose, leave a one-line note saying why — that converts a violation into a decision.
- **Voice: original and human, never house-imitation.** Steal the competitors' *craft* (reassure-then-pivot, worst detail last, dry closers, a personality behind the narration), never their sentences. The test: if a line could appear in any channel's video, rewrite it until it could only appear in ours.
- **The non-negotiable layer** (protects the channel, not the style — creative license never overrides these): canon facts + the hedging/numbers policy · the hypothetical-marker rule · anti-slop tells (fragment ladders, emotion labels, filler adjectives) · the approved roster, order, and CTA positions · no jokes on death beats · the source sheet.
- **The trade in one line:** a draft that's 90% on-system but alive beats one that's 100% compliant and flat.

The skeleton is M Simplified. The seasoning is Ficknime.

**Voice rules:**
- Write like you're telling a scary story to a friend. If a sentence wouldn't survive being spoken out loud across a table, rewrite it.
- Simple English. One idea per sentence. 8–16 words typical, with deliberate 2–4 word hammer sentences every few lines.
- Present the horror through concrete, drawable detail — never through adjective stacking. Banned filler words: *terrifying, horrifying, nightmarish, disturbing, creepy, entity* (max once each per FULL script, never twice in a section).
- Rotate section openers. Never start two creatures with the same pattern ("One night…" appears once per video, max).
- Escalation grammar: "At first… / Then… / As he got closer… / That was the mistake. / The next morning…" — vary across sections.

**Humor calibration (owner's brief + Ficknime's dry wit):**
- 1 small dry joke max per creature, and only in the closing line. 2–4 jokes total per video. Never during a death or attack beat.
- Style: deadpan observation, not slang. Good: "So the official survival plan for a thirty-foot child-eating monster is apparently just lock the windows and hope." Bad: "Bro is cooked. No rizz."
- Serious creatures (child victims, tragedy lore) get a creepy closer instead of a joke.
- **Joke-density note (added 2026-07-16):** Fickyep's ACTUAL current register is much jokier than this brief — nearly every section closes on a joke, and it uses casual roasts ("Bro is called Hexapod and still showed up with only two legs," "you're not the main character, you're just background scenery"). The owner's standing preference is still dry-wit-not-slang, so default to the restrained version — but a "jokes dialed up to match Fickyep" pass is a valid owner-selectable option for a given video. Ask if unsure; don't silently pick one.

**Confident physical description (added 2026-07-16):** describe each creature with specific, countable detail and a stated number (exact height, "11 red dots," "six arms," a body-part inventory), the way the competitor videos do — not vague ("a huge pale thing"). This is both a retention device and a big share of the section's word count. Numbers still follow the middle-path hedging rule: confident on canon figures, hedged on contested fan estimates.

**Two selectable narration modes** (pick per video, or per owner's request):
- **Mode A — "M Simplified classic":** third-person victim scenes, "To survive this creature…" blocks, both CTAs. Default for "…Explained in 9 Minutes" titles.
- **Mode B — "Ficknime hook":** second-person "You are… That is the ___." openers, punchline closers, minimal CTA. Better for lore-heavy topics (analog horror series, SCP deep dives).
- Blends are allowed: Mode A skeleton + Ficknime punchline closers is the house default.

**MODE B IS THE DEFAULT (owner decision, 2026-07-16).** Reasons: (1) it's where the niche's best current performers have moved (Fickyep, the 16-min Ficknime Doctor Nowhere video); (2) it's far easier on the editor — a second-person "you're driving home when the fog rolls in" needs generic, reusable footage (a road, fog), while Mode A's invented third-person scenes force the editor to visualize and cut a specific staged incident (a named witness, a specific house) that also breaks the edit every time the story is tweaked. Default to Mode B unless the title/format specifically calls for Mode A (see the adaptability rule). Mode B section shape: second-person hook → reveal → concept-first dramatic explanation → punchline/hammer closer. Hook length runs tight (~20–40 words), matching FK.

**THE ADAPTABILITY RULE — MATCH THE COMPETITOR, DON'T APPROXIMATE A HOUSE VOICE (governing principle, 2026-07-16).** The single biggest failure mode is writing to a generalized "house blend" from memory instead of to how the actual competitor for THIS title writes. We have measured, real transcripts — use them. In Step 0, after pulling the nearest competitor video(s), also extract and adopt THAT video's specific techniques, not a generic average. The technique kit is selected by evidence, per format and per universe:
- **Folklore / urban-legend titles** → use the ORIGIN ENGINE: each creature gets a tragic cause-and-effect backstory (betrayal / grief / injustice → transformation → curse). Confirmed present in Fickyep's Urban Legends video.
- **Doctor Nowhere (Silas Orion) thin-lore titles** → use the DN TOOLKIT (do NOT invent origins): concept/idea-is-the-horror, victim's-arc where canon gives one, unknown-as-payoff, aftermath-over-mechanism, relatable-feeling hooks, connection-web cross-links. Confirmed in the 16-min Ficknime DN video.
- **Giants / "Largest" titles** → escalate scale across the runtime, save the cosmic/biggest for late, place a friendly-but-ominous entry as a palate cleanser (e.g. Long Horse). Confirmed in the Fickyep/Ficknime giants videos.
The skeleton stays constant; the technique kit changes to match the evidence. When in doubt, open the nearest competitor transcript and write in ITS mechanics.

**Sentence-level dramatic-explanation craft (universal — use in every mode, reverse-engineered from the competitor transcripts):**
1. **Reassure-then-pivot:** state something safe, then pull it out ("it sounds harmless — but that's exactly what it wants").
2. **Real-time cause-and-effect chain:** narrate the encounter as "then… then… then," an escalating sequence, not a static profile.
3. **Worst detail last:** order information for impact, not logic — mildest first, the gut-punch at the end of the block.
4. **Reveal-within-reveal:** plant a detail, then peel it to expose something worse (the fake face peels back to the real one).
5. **Concept-first framing:** lead with the unsettling IDEA the creature embodies, not its measurements.
6. **Short declarative hammer** to close a build ("It was just the door.").
These are what make an explanation feel "dramatic" rather than informational. They cost nothing in honesty and belong in every script.

**HYBRID FORMAT IS THE DEFAULT (added 2026-07-16).** Do NOT force a full dramatized story onto every creature — that's M Simplified's move, not Ficknime/Fickyep's. Use full story (Mode A) only where the story is the selling point (a strong incident or emotional hook); use a compressed Ficknime hook (hook → reveal → lore → punchline, survival folded into the punchline) everywhere else, especially thin-lore creatures. This kills padding at the root: a thin-canon creature gets to be short instead of stretched into a scene it can't fill. Place the full-story anchors at the retention peaks (early gut-punch + finale run). The opener MAY be a compressed hook — a full story is not required for slot 1.

**HUMAN TOUCH IS MANDATORY IN EVERY MODE (added 2026-07-16).** Full-story sections follow one person through to their fate (attack / escape / disappearance) with a human beat — a choice, a memory, a hesitation (never an emotion-label like "a chill ran down his spine"). Compressed sections carry the human touch through SECOND-PERSON direct address ("the water tower at the edge of YOUR town"), not a scene. No section may be a cold camera pointed at a creature.

**PATTERN-BREAKS / FOURTH-WALL ASIDES (added 2026-07-16).** Allowed when a stretch goes flat — deploy at sag points (right after the CTA, the mid-video trough). Sparingly: a jolt overused becomes a gimmick.

## PART 4 — Video Blueprint

**Creature count is format-driven, not fixed.** Measured from the real videos:

| Format | Creatures | Words/section | Total words | Section style |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Story-driven monster list (MS style, 8–10 min) | 6–8 | 160–210 | 1,500–1,900 | Full story → definition → survival |
| Compressed monster list (FK style, 9–11 min) | 10–12 | 140–185 | 1,570–1,900 | Hook → reveal → lore → punchline |
| SCP/entity compilation (FK style, 16–21 min) | ~21 | 120–140 | 2,550–3,000 | Wiki-compact, no stories, no outro |
| Folklore/legends (MS style) | 7–8 | 180–210 | 1,600–1,700 | Story → appearance → origin block |

**The count for any given script comes from Step 0 of the pipeline (competitor calibration), never from assumption.** If the title promises "Every ___" or a compilation, the roster grows and sections compress. If it's story-driven, fewer creatures with full scenes.

**RUNTIME CALIBRATION — MATCH SECTION LENGTH TO CREATURE COUNT (added 2026-07-16, learned the hard way).** The section word budget is set by *how many creatures fill the runtime*, NOT by a fixed per-section number. Do not borrow a compressed per-section length from a high-count compilation and apply it to a low-count video — that was the v12 error (used the 21-in-16-min ≈ 120 w/section on an 8-creature video → it collapsed to ~6 min). Measured from real ~10-min competitor videos:

| Video (measured) | Duration | Creatures | Sec/creature | ≈ Words/section |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fickyep — Trevor Henderson Giants Pt.2 | 10:43 | 10 | ~64s | ~155–165 |
| Fickyep — FNAF's Most Disturbing | 10:44 | 10 | ~64s | ~155–165 |
| Ficknime — Doctor Nowhere Creepiest (compilation) | 16:04 | 21 | ~46s | ~115–130 |

So for a ~10-minute video: **10 creatures ≈ ~160 w each, OR 8 creatures ≈ ~200 w each.** Pick the creature count first, then the section budget follows. ~150–190 wpm narration.

**THE 6-BEAT SECTION ANATOMY (what actually fills ~160–200 words, reverse-engineered from the giants/FNAF videos).** A full section runs six beats, not four — the two most commonly dropped (and the reason short sections run thin) are #3 and #5:
1. **Cold open + "ordinary → it moves" turn** (~25–35 w): a mundane thing that then moves/steps/spreads wings.
2. **Concept or origin/context** (~20–30 w): the idea it embodies (DN thin-lore) OR its origin where canon supplies one (Trevor Henderson "collection period," FNAF murdered-child).
3. **Dense physical description WITH a confident, countable number** (~30–40 w): exact height, exact counts ("11 red dots," "six arms"), a body-part inventory — NOT "a huge pale thing." This beat carries a big share of the word count and is the one most often skipped.
4. **Behavior / how it kills** (~30–40 w), often with a hedged theory ("some believe… but that's never been confirmed").
5. **A famous-sighting / lore hook / connection or unknown-as-payoff** (~20–30 w): the standout incident, a cross-link to another creature, or a deliberate "what happens next is unknown."
6. **Joke or hammer closer** (~15–25 w).
Thin-lore creatures legitimately compress beats 2 and 5 (lean on unknown-as-payoff) rather than pad — that's adaptive, not a shortcut. Rich creatures carry all six and run longer. This is WHY the word count varies by creature.

**WORD COUNTS VARY BY CREATURE — NEVER UNIFORM (added 2026-07-16, reinforced).** Length follows lore depth + retention role: long opener, lean/sparse for thin canon, heavier for richest lore and the finale. Uniform per-section counts across a whole video are an AI tell — flag and vary. A natural contour (e.g., long open → two lean → sparse → tone-break bump → lean → two heavy closers) reads as authored; a flat line reads as generated. **Counts that are suspiciously round or near-identical (every section ~170, or a tidy 150/160/170 ladder) are themselves a tell — real transcripts are messy. Let sections land where the lore lands them (e.g., 48, 132, 71, 205), and do NOT round or pad to hit a target. Write the section, then report its actual count; never write to a pre-set number.**

**ORDERING IS RETENTION-BASED, NOT ARBITRARY (added 2026-07-16).** Strongest / most-differentiated hook first, thin creatures buried in the mid-video sag, tone-break in the trough, heavyweight finale, CTA after a peak. Save the marquee / most-anticipated creature for the finale and feature it on the thumbnail as the "promise" that keeps viewers to the end. Differentiation from the market leader is the tiebreaker when two slots are equal on retention (e.g., open on a strong creature the leader buries mid-list, rather than copying their opener).

Standard slot plan for a story-driven 8-creature video:

| Slot | Content | Words |
|---|---|---|
| Creature 1 | Strongest visual hook of the set — first 30 seconds decide retention. **The opener may run longest** (measured MS pattern: opening section ~235 words, later sections 185–210) | 185–240 |
| Creatures 2–3 | Next two strongest | 185–205 each |
| Mid CTA | After creature 3, never mid-story (MS-mode only) | 30–40 |
| Creatures 4–7 | Escalate size/stakes; a deliberate tone-break entry around slot 5–6 is allowed (FK pacing trick) | 160–200 each |
| Final creature | Best mystery or biggest scale — save a heavyweight for last | 185–210 |
| Outro CTA | Question to comments + subscribe + "Stay safe" sign-off (MS-mode; FK-mode ends cold on the last punchline) | 35–50 |

**Section beat sheet (every creature):**
1. **NAME.** (own line)
2. **Hook scene — MS mode: 90–140 words (measured from real transcripts, not the brief's 70–100 guess); FK mode: 25–40 words.** Ordinary person/POV → one wrong detail → closer → reveal → consequence. Every 1–2 sentences must be drawable.
3. **Definition pivot, 1 sentence.** "X is a [what it is] …" with size.
4. **Explanation, 40–70 words.** Signature feature → behavior → who it targets → best single piece of lore (one strong incident beats five weak ones).
5. **Survival or origin, 20–35 words.** "To survive this creature…" only when lore supports it; otherwise "There is no confirmed way to stop it…" For folklore: origin/meaning block instead.
6. **Closer, 1 line.** Dry punchline OR creepy final image. Alternate between the two across sections.

**Rotation tables (to avoid repetition):**
- *Characters:* driver, hiker group, security guard, photographer, married couple, night-shift worker, urban explorer, kid + parent, researcher, fisherman.
- *Settings:* empty highway, forest, bedroom window, abandoned factory, coastal road, rice field, apartment at night, campsite, small-town edge, tunnel.
- *First wrong detail:* flickering lights, a shape that shouldn't be there, a sound with no source, a photo anomaly, fog arriving too fast, a smell, a broadcast interruption, something standing too still.

**Visual notes: OFF by default (owner preference, 2026-07-16).** Do NOT put inline bracketed [VISUAL: …] cues in the narration unless the owner asks for them. All visual direction lives in the SEPARATE editor-materials file instead (see the editor-materials rule in Part 6), with each section tagged by shot type (🎬 full-story scene sequence vs ⚡ compressed hero shot) so the editor doesn't over-produce the compressed ones.

## PART 5 — Research Pipeline (what I do automatically when given a title)

**Step 0 — Competitor calibration (mandatory, never assume).** Before anything else, I check the competitor watchlist in `claude/competitor-intel.md` (Nonagon, ndog ceplok, Prumhy, Darkly, Sepele, Fickyep, Grimly, ExplainIt) plus M Simplified and Ficknime, find the actual videos they made for this title or the closest matching title — including analog horror titles — and pull their real transcripts. From them I measure: how many creatures they covered, which ones, in what order, words per section, which formula variant they used, and where CTAs sit. This sets the format targets for our script AND feeds the rotation log so our roster order, incidents, and openers provably differ from theirs. If no competitor video exists for the title, I calibrate from the nearest format in Part 4's table and say so in the delivery notes.

**Universe check (part of Step 0, added 2026-07-16): "Doctor Nowhere" = Silas Orion, a SEPARATE universe from Trevor Henderson.** The "explained" niche blends the two constantly (M Simplified's "Doctor Nowhere's BIGGEST Monsters" = Silas Orion creatures; the giant rosters people list are often Trevor Henderson's). Never mix the two rosters silently — pick a lane, confirm the attribution during research, and state it in the video's first seconds. If a reference image looks like a Trevor Henderson creature (Siren Head, Cartoon Cat, Long Horse, etc.) for a Doctor Nowhere title, it's the wrong universe.

**Step 1 — Decode the title contract.** "Biggest" = every creature must be huge. "Creepiest" = strongest imagery/lore wins. "Doctor Nowhere's" = only Doctor Nowhere (Silas Orion) creatures. "Every ___" = compilation mode, larger roster, compressed sections. If the roster can't honestly fill the title, I flag it before writing instead of padding.

**Title superlative note (added 2026-07-16): "Most Massive" is OFF the approved list** — it reads wrong for the niche. The proven scale words are **Biggest / Largest**. For a scale title, pair with "Creatures" (not "Giants," which is redundant with a scale word) to sidestep M Simplified's owned "Biggest/Largest Monsters." (Full superlative rotation lives in `competitor-intel.md`.)

**Scale-word honesty gate (added 2026-07-16).** For a "Largest / Biggest" title, every pick must be genuinely PHYSICALLY large. Cut power-of-threat entities whose bigness is conceptual, not physical (e.g., Father Fester — canon shows it in cramped, small spaces; its "eats everything, including time and space" hook is scale-of-threat, not scale-of-size → cut from a "Largest" roster, park it for a "Most Powerful Entities" video). Verify each pick's size against canon before locking; unverifiable creatures (no canon size/source) don't go in.

**Step 2 — Build the roster from what's proven to work.** I search related/popular/outlier videos in the niche to see which creatures audiences actually click on — *for selection and ordering only, never for facts or wording.* Roster rules: creature count from Step 0 calibration, each creature visually distinct from the others, ordered so scale/stakes escalate, heavyweight saved for last, optional tone-break entry mid-list.

**Step 3 — Deep research card per creature (non-negotiable).** Every creature gets real multi-source research before a single script word is written — original creator sources, fandom wikis, and series episodes cross-checked against each other. For thin-lore creatures, the research states what is unknown rather than padding. Sources in priority order:
- Doctor Nowhere / Trevor Henderson → the creator's original posts, captions, videos.
- Analog horror → the original series episodes (Gemini Home Entertainment, Local 58, Vita Carnis, Monument Mythos…).
- SCP → the official SCP Wiki article + test logs.
- Folklore → academic/folklore references, at least two sources.
- **Fandom wikis (villains.fandom, trevor-henderson.fandom, doctor-nowhere.fandom, backrooms.fandom, etc.) → full-fledged research sources.** They're often the best-organized lore compilations and where the community consensus (names, size estimates, timelines, connections) lives. Use them for appearance, behavior, incidents, and theories — cross-check against the original source when one exists, and label anything only the wiki says as *Fan-sourced* in the source sheet. When wiki and creator conflict, creator wins; when the creator is silent, the wiki version is used with a hedge ("fans describe…", "estimates place…").

Card fields: appearance / confirmed size / habitat / behavior / abilities / best incident / survival method / theories / unknowns. Empty field = "No confirmed information found," never an invention.

**Step 4 — Label every claim:** Confirmed canon · Supported interpretation · Fan theory/estimate · Dramatization. Labels live in the source sheet, not the narration.

**Hypothetical-marker rule:** when a dramatized scene carries real canon dates, incidents, or captions, the scene MUST open with a hypothetical marker ("Imagine…", "Picture this…") so invented framing can never borrow the credibility of confirmed lore. Fully invented scenes with no canon payload may stay third-person; scenes wrapped around real canon may not.

**Numbers policy:** confident delivery, honest sourcing. Creator-stated → "It stands around 23 meters tall." Fan estimate → "Estimates place it at around 23 meters, taller than a six-story building." Nothing → "Its exact size is unknown, but it towers over the tree line." Never invent dates, victims, locations, or reports. Dramatized scenes never carry fake dates ("In October 1996, five teenagers…" is banned unless canon).

**Step 4.5 — CHECKPOINT (mandatory stop — no writing until approved).** After calibration, roster research, and the research cards are done, I stop and present a decision summary before a single script word is written:

1. **Recommended creature count** for this title, with the calibration data that justifies it (competitor counts, words/section, runtime math) — plus the alternative (e.g., "7 deep vs 8 standard") and what each trades off.
2. **Proposed roster in order**, one line per creature: why it's in, its best confirmed lore hook, and its differentiation vs competitor rosters. Plus 2–3 bench alternates ready to swap in.
3. **Title check** — the exact final title + any stronger variant the data suggests.
4. **Mode choice** — A / B / blend, and where the CTAs and any twist/tone-break sit.
5. **Flags** — anything research surfaced: creatures with no canon source (cut or keep-with-hedge?), thin-lore creatures, canon ambiguities, overlap risks with a specific competitor video.
6. **The questions I need answered** — asked as clear multiple-choice decisions (creature count, roster swaps, title variant, joke density, anything flagged).

Only after the user confirms (or adjusts) do I write. If the user is unavailable and has pre-approved autonomous mode for a title, I proceed with the recommended defaults and mark every checkpoint decision in the delivery notes.

**Research mode note:** when the user enables research mode in chat, the deep research in Steps 0–3 is routed through it instead of manual multi-agent fetching (faster, cheaper). My job then is to hand research mode a tight brief: the exact creature list, the card fields to fill, the source priority order (creator posts → fandom wikis → series), and the labeling rules — then verify what comes back against the canon list before the checkpoint.

**Step 5 — Check the Rotation Log (anti-repetition + anti-copying).** Before writing, I read `claude/rotation-log.md` in this project; after every delivered script, I append to it. The log tracks, per script: creatures covered, opening pattern used per section, character types and settings used, closers/jokes used, and which competitor videos cover the same roster. Rules it enforces:
- Never reuse an opening pattern, character type, or setting combo from the last 2 scripts for the same creature type.
- "One night…" and every other opener template gets rest periods across videos, not just within one.
- No joke or punchline structure repeats across scripts.
- **Competitor-order check:** if M Simplified, Ficknime, or another explainer covered the same roster, our creature order, incidents chosen, and section openers must differ from theirs. Same format is the goal; same sequence or wording is plagiarism.
- Creatures already covered in a previous script get a fresh story angle (different witness, setting, and incident), never a re-skin of our own old section.

**Step 5.5 — PER-SECTION CRAFT STUDY (owner decision, 2026-07-17): learn from the competitor's treatment before writing, then diverge.** Generic "be creative" generation drifts toward the statistical average — the movie-trailer voice the anti-slop rules exist to catch. The antidote is grounding each section in a specific human writer's observable decisions on the same material. Per section, before writing:

1. **Pull both sources side by side:** the canon material for this creature (the creator's actual post/caption/image) AND the competitor's actual section covering the same creature (their real transcript, from Step 0's pulls).
2. **Study the gap — that's where the craft lives.** Canon gives a photo of four white legs in a manhole; the competitor turned it into a 60-second scene. How? What did they lead with, what did they hold back for last, what did they invent around the fact, which sentence made it land, where did they place the joke? Name the MOVE in one line (e.g., "withheld the size reveal until after the survival tip").
3. **Close the reference, then write from canon.** Apply the extracted move with a different execution — different scene, different angle into the same canon fact, different closer. Learn the technique, never mirror the sequence or wording.

**Convergence safeguard (mandatory):** studying a competitor section immediately before writing the same creature makes accidental echoing MORE likely, not less — the reference is fresh in context. So: the reference gets closed before drafting, and after drafting, the section is checked back against the reference for echoed structure, sequence, or phrasing (this stacks on the rotation-log competitor-order check). If the section tracks the reference's beat order or wording, rewrite the execution.

**Fallback for uncovered creatures:** when no competitor has covered a creature (fresh picks), use the nearest ANALOGOUS section — e.g., study how the competitor handled another screen/digital-type creature and borrow that move. Expect weaker lift from analogy; that's fine, don't force it.

**Traceability:** the delivery notes name each section's craft reference (competitor + video + the move extracted), or "no reference — analogous: X" — so the owner can see what was learned from where.

## PART 5.5 — Anti-AI-Slop Rules + Fan-Expert Voice (mandatory pass on every draft)

**The core law: every sentence must carry a new fact or a new image.** If a sentence exists only for rhythm or drama, cut it or merge it. The competitor transcripts prove the standard — M Simplified writes "At first, it didn't move. Then suddenly, its arm stretched forward and smashed through the window." Both sentences advance the scene. AI slop writes "It didn't move. Then it did. Not like an animal. Like a drawing." — three of those four fragments contain nothing.

**Banned patterns (the AI tells):**
- **Fragment ladders:** two or more consecutive fragments riffing on each other ("Not like an animal. Like a drawing." / "Floppy ears. Huge eyes. A grin."). ONE short impact sentence per section is allowed, and it must carry information ("Then it moved." after a stillness beat = fine, because it's the event).
- **Rhetorical setups:** "Here's the thing," "But here's where it gets worse," "And that's when everything changed," "Little did they know."
- **Emotion labels instead of images:** "her hands went cold," "a chill ran down his spine" → show the detail that causes the feeling instead. (This also bans emotion-DIRECTIVES like "that should be the first thing that scares you" — show it, don't tell the viewer to feel it.)
- **Ellipsis abuse and em-dash abuse:** max ~2 em-dashes per section, ellipses almost never.
- **Echo openers:** 3+ consecutive sentences starting with the same word; every section opening with the same grammatical shape. Also rotate the "One night / One evening / Late one night" time-marker template — max once per video.
- The test: read it aloud and ask "would M Simplified's narrator say this sentence?" If it sounds like a movie trailer, rewrite it as a person telling a story.

**ALLOWED (owner updates, 2026-07-16):**
- **"Not X, it's Y" reveal is ALLOWED** (removed from the ban list). It's a legitimate reveal device ("That glow isn't the sunrise. It's Sunman."). Use freely; just don't stack 3+ in one section.
- **Power-line details are allowed** for "this shouldn't exist" beats (e.g., a house with no road and no power lines).

**KEEP CREATURES MODULAR — NO THROUGH-LINE, NO NAMED-EMOTION CLOSERS (hard rule, learned 2026-07-16).** The proven M Simplified pattern is "hard cut to the next creature's name, zero transition sentences." Each creature is a standalone module. A connective narrative spine across creatures (e.g., "edge of town → foot of your bed" callbacks) or closers that spell out the fear in words ("every one of those alerts is a parent over an empty bed") are VIDEO-ESSAY moves — off-format for this compilation style. Closers stay single dry lines that leave the fear UNDER the surface ("a broken window nobody can explain" > "a parent over an empty bed"). *(This is the reason script v8 was reverted to v7/v9 on the Doctor Nowhere build.)*

**Fan-expert voice:** write as someone who has followed this niche for years and knows the canon cold — cites the actual caption, the actual date, the creator's actual number. This includes **correcting the niche's common mistakes in-script when it's earned**: "Plenty of videos invent heights for it — forty feet is the only number Henderson ever stated." One or two such corrections per video, max; they build trust and make the channel the authority, but stacked corrections turn the video into a lecture. Never name the competitor being corrected.

**Creator-citation budget (house voice rule):** the narration stays IN-WORLD by default, like M Simplified — the horror is narrated as if it's real, not as commentary about an artist's posts. **Maximum ONE explicit creator reference per section** (a name-drop, "the original post," "the caption said" — all count against the same budget), and it should be spent on the section's single strongest piece of canon: the verbatim quote, the fan-expert correction, or the killer caption. Every other sourced fact gets phrased in-world ("By all accounts, there is only one of it" / "He's a bottom feeder — too weak to cause disasters, so he nudges them") with its sourcing tracked in the source sheet, not the narration. Heavier citation density (MrUnnerving-style commentary) is not the house voice and needs explicit approval at the checkpoint.

**AUDIT COMMAND:** when the user says **"audit"** or **"reaudit"** (optionally naming a script or section), run the target through this full system: line-by-line anti-slop pass, voice comparison against real competitor transcript patterns, the Part 6 QC checklist, source-sheet verification, and rotation-log/calibration checks. Return (1) a findings list — line → problem → fix — and (2) the corrected version. "Reaudit" = repeat the pass on the fixed version until it comes back clean.

## PART 6 — Final QC Checklist

- Video starts with a creature name, not a greeting.
- Every creature matches the title's promise.
- No two sections open with the same pattern; "One night" used max once.
- Every sentence is speakable and most are drawable.
- Impact sentences present in every section ("Then it moved.").
- Banned adjectives each used ≤1 time per script.
- Jokes: ≤1 per section, 2–4 per video, only in closers, never on a death beat.
- Every number/date/incident traced in the source sheet; estimates hedged in-script.
- Mid CTA after creature 3; outro CTA ends with "Stay safe" sign-off (Mode A).
- Total 1,450–1,700 words; per-section word counts included.
- Rotation log checked before writing and updated after delivery; roster order and incidents verified different from competitor videos covering the same creatures.
- Step 0 competitor calibration done with real transcripts (creature count, section length, formula) — not assumed.
- Creature count matches the format table in Part 4 for this title type.
- Checkpoint presented and approved before writing (count, roster, title, mode, flags) — script matches what was approved.
- Anti-slop pass done: no fragment ladders, no rhythm-only sentences, no emotion labels or emotion-directives, em-dashes ≤2/section. ("Not X, it's Y" is allowed; just not stacked 3+ in a section.)
- Fan-expert voice present: at least one precise canon citation; ≤2 in-script corrections of common niche mistakes.
- **Hybrid format:** full dramatized story only where the story is the selling point; compressed FK hook everywhere else. No full story forced onto thin-canon creatures.
- **Human touch present in every section** — full-story sections follow one person to a resolved fate with a human beat; compressed sections use second-person direct address.
- **Word counts vary by creature** (not a uniform line); contour matches lore depth + retention role. Budgets are gravity, not quota — never pad or trim to hit a number.
- **Creative license applied:** each section shows at least one authored choice the template didn't dictate; any deliberate structural rule-break carries a one-line note; no line could appear verbatim in a competitor's video. License never used to bend the non-negotiable layer (canon, hedging, markers, anti-slop, roster/CTAs).
- **Craft study done per section (Step 5.5):** canon + competitor treatment compared, the move extracted and named in delivery notes, reference closed before drafting, and the finished section echo-checked against its reference (no mirrored beat order, sequence, or phrasing).
- **Creatures stay modular** — no narrative through-line across creatures, no named-emotion closers; closers are single dry lines with the fear left under the surface.
- **Universe is pure and stated** (Doctor Nowhere = Silas Orion, not Trevor Henderson — no silent blending).
- **Scale-word honesty:** for a "Largest/Biggest" title, every pick is genuinely physically large; conceptual-threat entities cut.
- **No inline [VISUAL:] cues** in the narration (owner preference); shot direction is in the editor-materials file, tagged 🎬/⚡.

**Every delivered script includes:** final title · full narration with creature headings and word counts · mid + outro CTAs · source sheet with claim labels · total word count. (Inline visual notes are OFF by default — all visual direction goes in the separate editor-materials file.)

**Editor materials rule:** every script ships with a SEPARATE editor-materials file (never inside the script itself) — one section per creature, in script order, containing: the link to the canon image source (wiki gallery page and/or creator post), which specific original image each hook/reveal references, design notes pulled from canon (colors, anatomy, scale, motion style), and ⚠️ warnings about the common fan-art mistakes for that creature (wrong anatomy, game-model versions, fan-made lookalikes). **Tag each section by shot type — 🎬 full-story scene sequence vs ⚡ compressed hero shot — so the editor doesn't over-produce the compressed ones.** Written so an editor completely new to the niche can build every visual without guessing or googling. Ends with a quick-links table and the creator's primary account handles.

**Versioning rule:** every script carries a version number — first delivery is **v1**; every change after that (audit fixes, user edits, section rewrites, roster swaps) bumps it: v2, v3, and so on. The version appears in the filename (e.g., `script-trevor-cannot-survive-v2.md`) and at the top of the document, together with a short **changelog** listing what changed since the previous version (line → what was changed and why). Never overwrite a version silently — the changelog is how the user tracks what moved between drafts.

## PART 7 — Demo: Same Creature, Both Modes (Watch Tower, lore verified from Doctor Nowhere canon)

**Mode A — M Simplified classic:**

> Watch Tower.
> One evening, a man was driving out of the city when he noticed something in the distance. At first, it looked like an old water tower — rusted, silent, forgotten. But he didn't remember it being there before. As he got closer, he saw it. A single, massive eye at the top, wide open, fixed on the road. He stopped the car. The air felt heavy. He stepped out anyway. The moment he crossed the city limits, his body began to come apart. No sound. No scream. Just pieces, fading into nothing. From a distance, the tower stood exactly where it had always been. And the eye kept watching.
> The Watch Tower is a structure-like giant from Doctor Nowhere's universe, resembling a rusted water tower with one enormous, unblinking eye at its top. Estimates place it at around fifty meters tall, though its true size has never been confirmed. It never moves. It doesn't need to.
> To survive it, stay inside the city limits while it is visible. Do not approach it. Do not meet its eye.
> Because this was never a tower built to store water. It's there to make sure nobody leaves.

**Mode B — Ficknime hook:**

> Watch Tower.
> You are driving home late at night when you spot an old water tower on the edge of town. Nothing unusual — until you realize there's a massive eye on top of it, and it's watching the city. That is the Watch Tower.
> It looks like a rusted, abandoned water tower, with estimates placing it somewhere between sixty and a hundred and sixty feet tall. The eye never closes. People who wander past the city limits while it watches don't come back — some are said to simply break apart, disintegrating into nothing. Doctor Nowhere himself hinted at the truth: it was never built to hold water. It exists to make sure no one leaves.
> So if you ever see a giant eye watching your town… congratulations. You live in a zoo now.
