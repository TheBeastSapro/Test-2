# What Vu actually does

*Measured from his publish cut on 2026-08-09, after the owner said the output
still read as amateur and told me to sharpen the axe before swinging it. He was
right. This document exists because I built two days of work on an assumption I
never measured.*

---

## The assumption that was wrong

I wrote, and then built on: *"Vu cuts about every 2.6 seconds, roughly 24 shots
in a 62 second section."*

I never measured it. I inferred it from his aggregate motion statistic and
stated it as fact.

Measured properly, by frame differencing at 10 fps over 130 seconds of real
sections:

| | Vu | my v8 cut |
|---|---|---|
| hard cuts per minute | **15.2** | 18.4 |
| mean gap between cuts | **3.99 s** | 3.05 s |
| **longest single hold** | **23.1 s** | 6.1 s |

**He cuts LESS often than I do.** And he will stay in one camera setup for
twenty-three seconds.

Note on method: ffmpeg's `select='gt(scene,N)'` reported zero cuts on BOTH
videos, including mine which has 26 known hard cuts. The detector was broken,
not the finding. Frame differencing is what works here, which is consistent
with the earlier discovery that pixel-delta cut detection fails on white-canvas
layouts.

## What he does instead of cutting

The Fetid King monitor sequence, 71.25s to 81.75s, sampled at 4 fps. This is
the beat the owner's own chat log calls the best thing anyone has made for the
channel.

| time | what changes |
|---|---|
| 70.00 | roster grid of all 8 creatures, punches IN to the Fetid King cell |
| 71.25 | a desk, a monitor, a dark teal room. Screen BLACK. Nothing happens |
| 73.00 | the monitor bursts into RGB glitch static. **Same framing** |
| 74.50 | the static settles into horizontal scan bars. **Same framing** |
| 77.00 | the creature appears INSIDE the monitor, small, buried in the scanlines |
| 79.25 | the camera pulls back, monitor smaller in frame, image still there |
| 80.50 | the image DUPLICATES. Two, then three copies inside the screen |

**Eleven seconds. One camera setup. Zero keyword pops.**

Every bit of change is *diegetic* - it happens inside the world of the shot. A
screen turns on. Static resolves. An image appears. It multiplies. Nothing is
achieved by cutting to a different picture.

## The three rules I had inverted

**1. Change comes from inside the scene, not from cutting between scenes.**
His 26.4% average frame change is generated by events within a held frame. I
reproduced the same number by cutting 26 times, which is why my cut measures
identically and reads as a machine. The metric was never the point.

**2. The scene is chosen for the lore, not as a backdrop.** The Fetid King
spreads through devices, so the scene IS a monitor and the creature appears ON
the screen. I was compositing a creature cutout onto whatever environment plate
I happened to have, which is set dressing, not storytelling.

**3. Restraint is the craft.** Eleven seconds with no text on screen but the
persistent title. My cut fires 12 keyword pops in 62 seconds, one every 5.2s,
plus icons. That is what "too much is going on" means, measured.

## What this means for the build

The generator currently optimises the wrong variable. It targets shot COUNT and
cut placement. It should target:

- **Scene count**, low. A 62 second section is perhaps 4 to 6 SCENES, not 26
  shots.
- **Within-scene event count**, high. Each scene needs a sequence of things that
  happen inside it: a light comes on, a shape resolves, something appears,
  something multiplies, the camera pulls back to reveal context.
- **Text density**, much lower. Pops are punctuation, not a running commentary.

That is a different data model from a shot list. A scene needs a timeline of
its own, and the assets need to support in-frame events rather than being
stills to cut between.

## Honest note on how this was found

Every wrong turn in this build came from the same failure: measuring an
aggregate, inferring a cause, and building on the inference without checking it.

- Motion 26.4% -> inferred "many cuts" -> built 26 shots. Wrong cause.
- "Cuts every 2.6s" -> never measured -> drove the entire rebuild. Wrong number.
- Clear-fraction gate passed inpainted plates -> looked at them, visibly smeared.
- Global alpha statistics passed a matte -> looked at it, limbs amputated.

The rule that would have caught all four: **measure the thing you are about to
build, not a number that correlates with it.** And look at the output before
shipping it, every time, the way you would look at a competitor's.
