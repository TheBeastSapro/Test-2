## SCRIPT

### Amber *(255 words)*

You hear your neighbor's lights flicker on a quiet street, and you step outside to look. Something tall is standing beside the house, completely still, waiting for something you haven't noticed yet.

That is Amber. Its whole design is built around one idea, a stare that doesn't come from a face, because Amber doesn't have one. Its reddish-brown limbs stretch far past any human shape, and by most accounts it stands well over two stories tall, high enough to look into an upstairs window without reaching. Where a head should sit there's nothing at all. Instead, a single eye rests in the center of its chest, and when it decides to feed, that eye splits open into a mouth wide enough to take a person whole.

Its fingers are long and strong enough to punch straight through glass without slowing down. Once it locks onto you it moves fast, closing distance the way an animal does, not the way a person would, and there's almost no gap between the moment it notices you and the moment it reaches the window. Most sightings cluster around fall and winter, always near houses with an uncovered or unlocked window, and almost always after dark.

Survivors describe the same detail every time, a moment of being completely unable to move once that single eye turns their way. If you see it standing outside, don't look back at it, and don't wait to see what it does next. Secure the glass before dark. Amber only needs one clear look inside.

### The Doctor *(204 words)*

You notice a tall figure in a formal suit standing somewhere it has no reason to be, and when you look straight at him, something about his face feels too familiar to explain.

That is the Doctor, the creature Doctor Nowhere named his whole account after. It appears as a slender, elongated figure, always dressed in something like a suit, with six long arms that reach down past its knees and a head crowned by spikes in a four-pronged shape. Its body is said to constantly change, but it always keeps one familiar face underneath. By most accounts it once ran with a pack of soul vultures, tearing at spirits and leaving suffering behind, before it grew fascinated with people instead.

Now it stalks the ones it has chosen, and witnesses say it turns up first in the edges of their photographs, standing a little further back in each one. Some accounts say it brings gifts or messages before the end, though no two people ever describe the same thing.

If a figure keeps appearing where it shouldn't, especially in your own photos, don't acknowledge it, and don't answer anything it seems to say. The Doctor doesn't stop once it's chosen who it's watching.

### Housewalker *(188 words)*

It starts as a slow, heavy dragging sound in the dark, getting closer. Then the ground shakes, and the house across the street begins to lean.

That is the Housewalker. Picture a hermit crab that decided a three-story house makes a better shell than anything it could grow, and you're most of the way there. The real creature stays hidden inside the structure, and the only parts you ever see are an eye and a mouth, watching through the broken windows if you're unlucky enough to be that close.

It doesn't run. It moves slow and heavy, dragging the whole house along with it, groaning against the ground as if it's deciding where to go before it commits. Whatever house it's wearing gets taken along for the walk, and whatever was inside that house when it started moving doesn't stay untouched for long.

The advice here is simple, and it isn't clever. If you see a house that's moving, you leave the area. Don't get closer for a better look, and don't wait to see which way it turns. Distance is the only thing that has ever worked.

### Sunman *(202 words)*

You're outside with a friend on an ordinary afternoon when the sunlight starts to feel wrong, too heavy, too close, pressing down instead of just warming you.

That is Sunman. No one has pinned down an exact height, but every account describes the same shape, an impossibly thin body with several long limbs and a star-shaped head fixed in a wide smile over small, deep-set eyes. Those limbs look built for reaching and holding, though nobody has confirmed what it does once it has you, or how close it gets before you notice it at all.

That's the whole trap of it, a shape that hides in the one thing you can't avoid outdoors, the light itself. It only shows up in open daylight, and people describe the same warning every time, the afternoon going strangely heavy, like the sun is leaning on their shoulders. Doctor Nowhere has left it deliberately unclear whether Sunman stands for warmth and life or for heat and exhaustion.

There's no confirmed way to survive it, because there's no confirmed account of anyone who has. If an ordinary afternoon starts feeling heavier than it should, the only instinct worth trusting is to get out of the light.

### The Birthday Giant *(208 words)*

Out past the fence line, while you're stringing up decorations in the backyard, something tall and thin is standing there, patient, like it's waiting to be asked in.

That is a Birthday Giant. They're lanky, neckless creatures with a stark black head on a pale or tan body, and no two heads look alike, some ending in something like a crown, others tapering off like a bare tree. Fan sketches place them around 11 meters, roughly 38 feet, though that figure was never confirmed and comes from lining them up against the people standing nearby.

They gather just outside old towns and wait for a birthday celebration to start. Once the party begins they expect to be included, and they get loud and demanding about it if they're ignored, less a monster and more an "almighty jerk," in Doctor Nowhere's own words. The idea behind it is stranger than the creature, that it's mostly just lonely and wants to be acknowledged. Leave one out long enough and it simply wanders off toward the next town over.

So maybe don't skip the invite. A Birthday Giant that feels seen is the easiest thing on this entire list to deal with, no distance, no locked windows, just a little attention.

### Driving Down the Highway *(180 words)*

You're alone on the highway at night when fog rolls in fast, thick enough to swallow your headlights whole. Something is moving through it, low and fast, on all fours, matching your speed without trying.

That is Driving Down the Highway. Estimates place its body at around 27 meters, close to 89 feet from nose to tail, carried on long slender legs with a back arched high above the road. It doesn't run so much as glide, covering ground in strides that shouldn't be possible for something that size, drifting alongside the car like the fog itself decided to move.

No one has ever gotten a clean look at its face, and it seems to like it that way. Every account ends the same, someone driving fast, refusing to stop, and refusing to check the mirror again once they've made it home.

If dense fog drops onto an empty road at night, don't test how long you can outrun whatever's inside it. Turn the car around, keep your eyes forward, and don't stop until the fog is behind you completely.

### The Boiled One *(229 words)*

Late one night, a rerun of an old kids' show glitches on your TV, and a huge, dark figure with a melted red face fills the screen, speaking in a voice far too calm for what it's saying.

That is the Boiled One, filed under the designation PHEN-228. It can reportedly change its own height at will, which is part of why witness estimates never line up, but it's always described the same way, an enormous, gaunt mass with raw crimson skin, a misshapen head, a wide mouth packed with teeth, and thin wire-like strands where hair should be. A single pulsing orifice sits in its chest, and no one has ever explained what it's for.

The danger isn't its hands, it's your memory. This is a cognitohazard, which means once you've clearly seen its face, that image is stuck in your head for good, and people report hearing trumpets and something like laughter or screaming in the nights that follow. The worst part waits until you fall asleep. Exposure has been described as triggering a pseudocoma, leaving the body paralyzed but still alive.

If a broadcast glitches into something that shouldn't be on air, turn it off the first time. Don't watch it twice to be sure of what you saw, and don't fall asleep right after. Some things follow you into the quiet part of the night.

### Watch Tower *(229 words)*

You're standing at the edge of town at dusk when something on the horizon catches your eye. It looks like an old water tower, the kind nobody bothers fixing anymore, standing where you're sure there wasn't one before.

That is the Watch Tower, and it's the largest thing on this list. It isn't rusted metal left over from another decade, it's something that decided this is where it lives now. Estimates put it at around 51 meters, close to 167 feet, and at the very top sits one massive eye that never closes and never looks away once it's found you. The surface beneath looks weathered and metallic, but no one has ever confirmed what it's actually made of.

The tower doesn't chase, and it doesn't need to. It just watches from a distance, patient in a way nothing that size should be able to afford. Doctor Nowhere has said on his own socials that it's a water tower that disintegrates anyone who crosses beyond the city limits while it's watching, quietly, no sound and no warning, until there's nothing left where you were standing.

So if you notice something tower-shaped on the horizon that wasn't there last week, don't drive toward it out of curiosity, and don't leave the city limits while it's still in view. Stay where the lights are on, and let someone else go check.
