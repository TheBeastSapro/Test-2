## NARRATION

### 1. Cartoon Cat (~235 words)

Cartoon Cat.

Around three in the morning, a security guard was finishing his rounds beside an abandoned mall when he noticed something standing in a doorway. It was tall and completely black, with white gloves and a wide smile — like a mascot someone forgot in the 1980s. At first, it didn't move. Then its arm stretched the full length of the parking lot without making a sound, bending in ways an arm never should, and the smile never changed. He ran. Behind him, the thing folded itself through the doorway like rubber. The next morning, the building was searched. Nothing was there.

**[VISUAL: black cartoon figure grinning around a doorframe of a dead mall; arm stretching across a parking lot]**

Cartoon Cat looks like a cat pulled straight out of a 1930s cartoon — pitch-black body, white gloves, red gums, and a grin full of crooked teeth. Its size changes between sightings, because its body follows cartoon logic instead of physics. Underneath, it's something ancient wearing the cartoon like a worn-out costume, and as long as people keep watching, it has a door into our world. Ask why it hasn't caused a massacre yet, and the lore gives you one answer: every year, people go missing.

To survive Cartoon Cat, stay out of abandoned buildings, especially in the dead hours after 3 a.m. Weapons only make it curious.

Henderson was once asked which of his creatures is worse — Cartoon Cat, or the Man with the Upside-Down Face. He said Cartoon Cat. You're about to meet second place.

### 2. The Sewer Spider (~190 words)

The Sewer Spider.

On his first week underground, a new city worker joked to his supervisor about the alligators people say live in the sewers. The old man laughed. Then he stopped. "It's not alligators," he said. "It's spiders." That warning is almost word for word how this creature was introduced — through a post written as the testimony of a former city worker who knew exactly what was down there.

**[VISUAL: open manhole at night; four long white legs unfolding out of it]**

The only known photo shows four glossy white legs rising out of an open manhole — each one longer than a man, bending at three separate joints, covered in fine spikes. The rest of the body has never been seen. It was never even given an official name; "Sewer Spider" came from fans. And if you read the original post closely, the worker never describes one spider. He says spiders.

To survive them, stay out of storm drains and away from open manholes. If a cover is off and there's no crew around it, cross the street.

And the next time your city closes a road for "maintenance" — maybe don't ask what kind.

### 3. The Man with the Upside-Down Face (~225 words)

The Man with the Upside-Down Face.

Imagine scanning your grandmother's old photo albums and noticing the same man in three different pictures. A New Year's party at an air force base in 1943, where a man was fatally injured. A car accident in 1948. A train disaster in 1951. Decades apart, the same black suit, standing calmly in the background. You zoom in. In every one of those photos, his head is attached upside down. And he is smiling.

**[VISUAL: old photos side by side, same suited figure in each background; zoom to inverted smiling face]**

Those three photographs are the creature's real origin, and the original post carries its most disturbing detail: no matter how close he stands to people, nobody ever remembers seeing him. He only exists in the pictures afterward. He's been traced back to a marketplace photo from 1910s Hungary, always near tragedy, never causing it directly. He's a bottom feeder — too weak to create a disaster, so he nudges one along instead. A tire pops. An argument turns violent.

There is no confirmed way to survive him, because you will never see him. He's only ever found in photos — and in newer images, he appears in pictures taken months *before* the tragedy.

So go ahead. Check your camera roll. If he's there, it's already scheduled.

### 4. The Highway Worm (~205 words)

The Highway Worm.

A driver caught a flat on an empty highway just after midnight. While he crouched by the wheel, the underbrush across the road shifted. He aimed his flashlight at it. Between the branches, something pale was coiled around itself — and along the top of it, a row of teeth. Human teeth. He left the car on the jack and drove on three tires until the sun came up.

**[VISUAL: dark highway shoulder; flashlight beam finding pale coils and a toothy maw in the brush]**

The Highway Worm is a massive serpent with pale, calloused skin and a mouth full of unsettlingly human teeth. Unlike its smaller cousin, it doesn't bother wearing a false face. Its true size has never been confirmed — fan estimates put it around thirty feet. Many believe the Bridge Worms lurking under overpasses are actually its young, though that theory has never been confirmed. What is known is what happens to people who get close: the Bridge Worm's own photo caption ends with "RIP Photographer Tomas Pearcing, missing and presumed dead."

To survive it, never stop on a dark shoulder. Drive on the rim to a lit area — the car can be replaced.

Someone once asked Henderson how to kill one of these worms. His answer: nobody's managed it yet, but you could always try. Good luck.

### 5. Cartoon Dog (~190 words)

Cartoon Dog.

Two urban explorers were filming inside a condemned house when their camera caught something in a doorway at the end of the hall. It had floppy ears, huge white eyes, and a grin of human teeth stretched across a black, rubbery face. It looked almost friendly until they noticed its limbs were attached in the wrong places.

**[VISUAL: dark hallway; cartoon dog grinning in the far doorway, arms bent wrong]**

Cartoon Dog looks like a cartoon mascot from the same poisoned universe as Cartoon Cat — and by all accounts they really are two of a kind, the only known members of the same species. It has only ever appeared in two official images, both in ruined buildings. Almost nothing about it is confirmed: no size, no origin, no weaknesses. Just one caption under its first photo: "they're just like your favorite cartoon." The word doing all the damage in that sentence is "they're."

There is no confirmed way to survive it. If a cartoon smiles at you from a doorway, you leave — through a wall if necessary.

Henderson put a question mark next to Cartoon Dog's name on his own creature list. Even its creator isn't sure what it is.

### 6. Siren Head (~230 words)

Siren Head.

A woman visiting an old cemetery noticed a rusted pole on the hill — tall as a telephone pole, standing where no telephone pole should be. Then it straightened up. Two air-raid sirens turned toward her like a face, and they screeched to life, blasting broken words across the graveyard: *NINE. EIGHTEEN. ONE. CHILD. SEVENTEEN. REMOVE. VILE.* Then it started down the hill toward her. That scene isn't fan fiction — it's the creature's very first recorded appearance.

**[VISUAL: cemetery at dusk; rusted siren-headed giant unbending from a pole shape, striding downhill]**

Siren Head stands about forty feet tall. Plenty of videos invent bigger numbers for it, but forty feet is the height Henderson actually stated. Its body looks mummified, the color of rust and dead bark, and instead of a head it has two sirens that can broadcast anything: emergency alerts, static, music, and the voices of people you love, mimicked perfectly to pull you off the trail. It can stand motionless for days pretending to be a pole. Victims are found with their eardrums and eyes burst. And by every account, there is only one of it — it's simply everywhere it needs to be.

To survive Siren Head, distrust any familiar voice calling from the woods. If a siren has no source, don't search for it. And don't count on your car — it keeps up.

The lore describes it as an ambassador for something else, from somewhere else. Nobody knows what it's announcing. It hasn't finished yet.

### 7. Long Horse (~215 words)

Long Horse.

The last creature on this list cannot hurt you. That is not good news.

A girl kept hearing something in her dreams — a soft, dry crack, like a joint popping, coming closer every night. Then one evening she heard it while she was awake, from the hallway. Around her door frame bent a horse's skull with no lower jaw, on a neck that kept going and going, vertebrae like giant human fingers, disappearing down the stairs and around the corner and, as far as anyone can tell, forever.

**[VISUAL: horse skull peeking around a bedroom door, endless finger-boned neck trailing into darkness]**

Long Horse is one of the only creatures its creator has personally marked one hundred percent friendly. Its neck is genuinely infinite. It travels through dreams, it likes music and apples, and for whatever reason, it smells like cinnamon. It appears for exactly one purpose: to warn. Seeing it means misfortune is coming soon — not because of Long Horse, but because of whatever comes after it.

So yes, you will survive Long Horse. It showed up to make sure of that. Whether you survive what it came to warn you about is a different question.
