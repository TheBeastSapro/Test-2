## Siren Head

You are walking beside a forest road when an emergency alert starts playing somewhere beyond the trees. The message cuts off halfway through a sentence.

Then your own voice answers it.

That is Siren Head.

It stands around forty feet tall, with a narrow, mummified body the color of rusted metal. Its arms hang almost to the ground. Two loudspeakers sit where a head should be, joined to the neck by cables, flesh, and black wires.

It has no visible eyes, but it still watches.

The speakers imitate radio broadcasts, warning sirens, private conversations, and people calling for help. That sound is not background noise. It is bait.

Siren Head waits among trees, telephone poles, and streetlights until someone walks close enough to realize that one of them has joints.

Then it moves.

It is heard more often than seen, using stolen voices and false emergency signals to separate victims from the group. Anyone who follows the sound disappears without a trace.

Nobody knows exactly what Siren Head is. The tower of rust and skin may only be the piece of it that fits inside a camera frame.

There is no confirmed method for killing it. Running only matters before it reveals itself, because once the hunt begins, it can cross an open field in seconds while hiding every footstep beneath its own broadcasts.

If a missing friend calls from deep inside the woods, do not answer.

Siren Head may already know why you would.

## Good Boy

You are staying in a hotel when something begins pacing through the hallway.

Four steps.

A pause outside one door.

Then four more.

By the time it stops outside your room, the handle is already beginning to move.

The creature outside your room is Good Boy.

Good Boy is not a dog.

It walks on four long legs, with pointed ears, a narrow body, and black fur that disappears into darkness. But its face is wrinkled and almost human. Its cloudy eyes have no pupils, and its mouth hangs open without a single tooth or tongue inside it.

The creature does not bark or scratch.

It listens.

Good Boy walks through dark hallways at night, stopping outside each room and tilting its head toward the door. It waits for movement on the other side: a footstep, a whisper, the click of someone reaching for the lock.

Then it waits for you to open it.

One photograph captured Good Boy standing in a hallway even though the creature was not there when the picture was taken. That means it does not need to enter a building normally. It was already inside before anyone thought to check.

Nobody knows what happens after the door opens.

But Good Boy's name was later crossed out.

So when something sits patiently outside your room, do not mistake patience for obedience.

A good boy waits for permission.

This one waits for the door.

## Costume Man

You are driving home after midnight when your headlights catch something blue standing in the road.

At first, it looks like a mascot helping an injured person. Then its arm moves, the body scrapes across the asphalt, and the mascot slowly turns toward your car.

The creature is known as Costume Man, or Mr. Mascot.

It towers over the person hanging from its hand. Blue fur covers its swollen body, while two thin antennae rise from the top of its head. Its mismatched googly eyes stare in different directions above a wide, lipless mouth. The legs are short and heavy, but the arms are unnaturally long, hanging almost to the ground and ending in hands large enough to carry an adult without effort.

Costume Man was once an ordinary performer who died inside the suit. By the time anyone opened it, the body had rotted too far for an autopsy to explain what killed him.

But the costume did not remain empty evidence.

It began walking again, with the dead man still sealed somewhere inside it.

When a mascot appears where no celebration is taking place, keep driving.

The person inside the costume is already dead.

The person in its hand is next.

*(mid-CTA)* If you have survived the first three creatures, subscribe now. It will not protect you from the next five, but at least the algorithm will know where to send the search party.

## The Man with the Upside-Down Face

You take a photograph after a highway collision. The wreckage is obvious, paramedics are working, and a crowd has gathered around the car. But when you examine the image later, there is one person nobody remembers seeing.

A thin man in a dark suit stands beside the wreck, smiling with his entire face upside down.

That is the Man with the Upside-Down Face.

He appears wherever suffering has already begun, and he has been doing this for a long time. He is there in a party photo from 1943, beside a wrecked car in 1948, at the edge of a train disaster in 1951. Witnesses never notice him at the scene. Cameras do. In photographs, he stands close to the injured and grieving, staring directly at the lens as if he knew exactly when the picture would be taken.

The Man with the Upside-Down Face feeds on misery. He is drawn to fear, pain, arguments, and sudden death, but he does more than observe. A damaged tire or a small disagreement becoming violent may be enough to create the tragedy he wants.

You cannot confront something you cannot see. By the time he appears in a photograph, the suffering has already fed him.

After an accident, check every picture.

If he is smiling in the background, he may be waiting for the next one.

## The Fetid King

There is an image that refuses to stay deleted.

Every time you remove it, two more copies appear. The files spread across your desktop, each one showing the same skeletal figure wearing a jagged wooden crown. Then the pictures begin changing. The skull turns toward the camera, the power flickers, and the filename becomes kingbirth.jpg. The capital letters scattered through the copies spell HAIL KING.

The Fetid King does not haunt houses. It infects devices.

Its body is a stretched human skeleton with only seventeen ribs, an open mouth, and thin strands of hair clinging to the skull. It moves through computers like a living virus, corrupting programs, multiplying its image, and forcing itself closer with every new file.

Once the infection begins, deleting the pictures does nothing. Restarting the computer does nothing. Even shutting the screen only buys time.

Nobody knows exactly how the Fetid King kills, or whether it ever leaves the computer at all. The screen fails first, the power follows, and the person using it disappears soon after.

So if kingbirth.jpg appears on your desktop, do not search the image.

You are not identifying the file.

You are announcing that you have seen the King.

## Country Road Creature

You take the back road because it is faster. No houses, no streetlights, and no other cars, only your headlights cutting through the trees.

Then something crosses the road in a single stride.

The Country Road Creature is a pale, hairless humanoid with limbs several times longer than its torso. It usually runs on all fours, bending its elbows, knees, spine, and neck at angles that make every movement look broken.

Seeing it beside the road is only the beginning.

The creature can keep pace with a moving vehicle, vanish beyond the headlights, and reappear close enough to touch the glass.

Estimates put it around twenty feet tall when it stands, yet it can fold that frame into something almost human, waiting beside the road like a stranded traveler.

So when a lone figure raises its hand on an empty country road, do not slow down.

Check where its arms end first.

By then, it will be running beside you.

## Bridge Worm

Under the interstate, between the support beams, something pale is watching you walk past. Its face is almost harmless, with two black eyes and a narrow mouth stretched across white skin.

Then the face folds backward.

Bridge Worm is a long, flesh-colored predator that nests beneath bridges, tunnels, and overpasses. Two pairs of nine-foot arms hold its body above the ground, each ending in flexible, bone-like fingers. The white face at the front is only a flap of skin, used to conceal red tissue and thirty-six human-like teeth underneath.

It usually hunts at night, remaining inside abandoned places where passing footsteps are easy to hear. When prey becomes scarce, it leaves the bridge and searches farther from its nest. The false face stays closed until the creature is close enough to feed.

A photographer captured one unfolding that mask. He disappeared and was presumed dead.

So if something pale watches you from beneath a bridge, do not approach because it looks frightened.

The expression on its face is not fear.

It is packaging.

## Cartoon Cat

You enter an abandoned mall and hear music playing through speakers that stopped working years ago. The tune is old-fashioned and warped.

At the end of the corridor, a cartoon cat steps from behind a shuttered store.

Cartoon Cat has a rubber-hose body, white gloves, round eyes, and a permanent smile filled with human-like teeth. Its limbs stretch far beyond their normal length, bending and twisting without bones. It can flatten itself, change its size, and reshape its body with the freedom of an animated character. It can squeeze beneath doors, climb across ceilings, and reach through rooms without bringing the rest of its body closer.

But the cartoon appearance is only a disguise.

Behind that smiling face is something ancient, hollow, and hungry. It seems to use old media as its doorway, wearing the shape of a forgotten mascot because people still recognize it. When it moves, witnesses describe it like bad animation: frames missing, a limb arriving before the rest of the body.

Cartoon Cat does not follow the rules that limit flesh, distance, or injury. Hiding inside the mall only gives it more walls to bend around.

The moment cheerful music starts playing in an empty building, leave before the chorus repeats.

The show has already started.

And Cartoon Cat does not need an audience that survives.

*(outro)* If you survived all eight monsters, subscribe before one of them notices the witness count is still going up. And watch the next video, because apparently making better decisions was never part of the plan.
