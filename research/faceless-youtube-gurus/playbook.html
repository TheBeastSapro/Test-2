We’ve helped 100+ YouTubers blow up their channel. And from our experience, 80% of what causes a channel to blow up is the ideas! So here’s the 13 step ideation checklist we follow that pretty much guarantees viral videos👇

  1. Feasible - Can you realistically make this video well? Even if you have the best idea in the world, if you can’t execute the actual content well (whether it be budget, time, resources or any other reason), then it’s not a good idea for your channel at this time. But save it - maybe in the future you’ll be able to make it. (Alongside each of these points in the checklist, we’ll also be showing real client results from working with the 1of10 team to prove that this ideation method actually works!)
  2. Positive Data - Is there evidence that this idea would perform well? What videos can you find on YouTube (or potentially outside of YouTube) to suggest that there’s a high interest in this idea. (The video in this graph was inspired from outside the niche, and it absolutely crushed for the creator becoming one of their most viewed videos of all time)
  3. Negative Data - Is there recent evidence that this idea isn’t working well? Just like how you should look for positive data, it’s also important to look for reasons not to make an idea. Are there recent videos on similar topics that underperformed despite having good content? (We’ve been working with this creator for roughly 1 year, and in that time the views have increased by 50%, despite reducing output!)
  4. Unique - Can you do it first, escalate, twist or improve on what’s out there? It’s never good to directly copy on YouTube, so ideally your idea is unique in some way. This can be through taking inspiration from outside your niche, twisting ideas, improving on what’s already out there etc. We’ll be doing a full breakdown on all the ways you can do this soon!
  5. Title - Can this idea be clearly described in 10 words or less? While not every title needs to be fewer than 10 words, if an idea takes more than 10 words to explain, it’s usually too complicated and oftentimes not a good idea. Of course, as with everything on this list, there are exceptions!
  6. Thumbnail - Can you visually build curiosity around this idea in a thumbnail? This is really important, and often something even experienced creators leave til too late. But having a clear idea of 2-3 thumbnail variations you can test for each video before filming is a cheat code for choosing the best ideas. If you can’t package the video well, then it’s not a good idea for YouTube.
  7. Content - Is there enough interesting content behind this idea? This is trap many creators fall into, but they’ll have an idea that sounds great on paper, but when it comes to actually filming the idea, the content itself is boring or just doesn’t work. Always think through what the actual content behind an idea would look like in this stage.
  8. Hook - Can you actually create an intriguing hook for this video? This is a niche one, but can you actually write a hook for this video that delivers on the packaging and sets up the rest of the video without ruining the payoff?
  9. Goals - Does this video fit the longterm goals of your channel? This one is super important. Just because a video can get a lot of views doesn’t necessarily mean you should make it. Does this video bring the right audience and take your channel closer to where you want it to be in 5 years time? A viral video with the wrong audience can do more harm than good!
  10. TAM - Is there a large enough audience for this idea? This is a simple one, but it’s super important to check that there’s actually a large enough TAM for your idea to meet your goals. A quick way to assess this is to find the highest viewed videos covering the same topic / similar topics and assume that is roughly the maximum TAM for your content.
  11. Audience - Does this video appeal to you core audience, casual viewers and new viewers? If your goal is to grow, it’s important to make sure that every video appeals to your regular viewers, but also a new viewer seeing your channel for the first time could also watch and enjoy the content.
  12. Risk - Are there any audience, reputation, timing or relevance concerns? This is typically not an issue, but a good thing to check just in case. Could there be any potential backlash from you making this idea that you’re not comfortable with?
  13. Seasonality - Is this the best time to post this video? Is this the best time to post this video, or would it perform better if you posted it at some other point in the future? Or maybe this idea would have crushed 2 months ago, but now it’s not a good time to make.
The one thing every viral video has in common1 of 10 · 11 steps

I’ve worked on videos totalling billions of views across hundreds of channels. And the one thing every viral video has in common? A great idea. So here’s the exact frame work I use to find banger ideas (in ANY niche)👇

  1. Before we go deeper, it’s important to understand that every viral idea can be broken down into 2 core components (and technically a 3rd as well, but more on that later). The Interest Topic This is essentially what the video is about. This will depend on what your audience is actually interested in. In the weapons niche this could be ‘Katana’. In the the fitness niche, this could be ‘burning fat’ Each interest topic will have a different Total Addressable Market (TAM), but also a different level of vitality, which is based on the TAM, supply of content already out there, and it’s relevance right now. While interest topics can fluctuate over time, especially due to over-saturation or external factors, they’re generally consistent and often times you can use older data to support this.
  2. The idea format The format is essentially the framing of the interest topic / the structure of the video. ‘$1 vs $1,000,000’ - which is essentially showing contrast between 2 extremes. ‘Coldest vs Hottest’ or ‘Biggest vs Smallest’ would essentially be the same format. ‘Give Me 9 Minutes, and I’ll show you how to ____’ - an example from the education niche. The format dictates the structure of the video and framing of the topic. It’s usually less of an indicator of TAM, but can be heavily affected by the supply of other videos (i.e. if a lot of videos have been done in a certain format, then it may lose it’s novelty over time), and so relevance is super important here.
  3. To have the best chance of coming up with a viral idea, we want to find data-backing for BOTH the interest topic and core format of the idea. This will give us a strong indication of whether an idea will perform well. The key here is to not take inspiration all from one place, but to mix and match different pieces of data and combine them into one unique idea that has support from several different data points. To do this, there are 5 main ways we can find inspiration for these, and you should be looking in all of these places.
  4. Inside your own channel This is going to be the strongest indication for which interest topics and formats work well, and which ones don’t. After all, this is data from your exact audience. A great example of this is what we did with one of our clients, Mike Shake. 2 years ago, we made a idea where Mike tested ‘How Dangerous is a Whip?’ At the time, this video crushed and was one of his most viewed videos. So 1 year later, we created another video around the same interest topic, Whips, but this time Mike built a crazy dangerous whip. The result? 14 Million views.
  5. Inside your niche One of the easiest ways to find winning ideas is by looking at what’s already overperforming inside your niche. Here you’re looking for formats and interest topics. In fitness, for example, supplements and performance enhancers is an interest topic that consistently produces huge outliers and had a huge TAM. This led us to this outlier for Will Tennyson, ‘I Tried EVERY Legal Performance Enhancer’. But we actually found the format for this idea from the next step which is…
  6. Inside Adjacent Niches Adjacent niches are essentially niches that share either a large audience overlap, topic overlap, or audience characteristics overlap. A good way to find adjacent niches is to think of your niche as a topic on a family tree. Let’s use day trading as an example. You want to find the ‘parent’ topic for your niche, which in this case could be money / finance. Then, you want to find the other topics under that main parent niche. Essentially finding the sibling topics for your niche. In this case, that could be topics like ‘investing’ or ‘crypto’ etc.
  7. Adjacent niches are generally best for finding formats. You can sometimes get an idea for interest topics here, but often times these would be different due to the fact that the niches and topics covered are completely different. Here’s an example we helped to ideate for Jdub, taking inspiration for the format from the Youtube education niche, and adapted it into the trading niche.
  8. Broader YouTube Searching broader YouTube can be a great way to find fresh formats that haven’t been brought into your niche yet. But here it’s even more important than in any of the previous steps to really understand exactly why a format or idea worked so well, in order to find out whether that might translate into your niche or what you need to adapt. We recently did this in the fitness / weight loss niche, by taking inspiration from an investing video. ‘The [Something] Secret to [Desired Outcome]’
  9. External But YouTube isn’t the only place you can find inspiration for ideas. You can also look outside of YouTube to find both interest topics and formats. For example, you could find which topics have a super high interest on Reddit, TikTok, Instagram or even Google search prediction. You could also pull popular formats used on TV shows and bring those over to YouTube. And this can apply to thumbnails as well. Here’s an example of an idea and thumbnail I came up with for Ohara, where I was actually inspired from a Google search recommendation and a scene in Naruto.
  10. But the real key here is to understand the psychology behind why each of these formats or interest topics performed so well in the first place in order to find out how you can adapt them without losing the core substance that made them perform so well in the first place. For example, taking the $1 vs $1,000,000 framing as an example. Why did this format work so well? Humans love contrast, and this takes it to the extreme. It shows the absolute cheapest vs most expensive element in a given interest topic. ‘Money’ and ‘Expensive’ things are a super high universal intrigue point across YouTube and content in general. Retention optimised structure. The structure of this idea leads to the biggest payoff (the $1,000,000 item) at the end of the video. So viewers have to watch til the end to see the highest interest point.
  11. But there’s also a third thing to look out for, and this is viral vectors. Viral vectors (or ‘viral vextors’) are essentially things that are of high interest across different niches. For example, ‘Japanese’ or ‘Invisible’. These are not restricted to one particular niche, and are used to supplement an interest topic and make it extra enticing. They don’t always fit in every idea, but can be used to take a good idea, and make it amazing.

How to come up with BANGER video ideas 📈 Let's break down the entire YouTube ideation process (bookmark) 🧵👇🏼

  1. STEP 1: OUTLIER RESEARCH (Foundation) Use a tool like @1of10media or do it manually.   3. STEP 3: COMPETITION ANALYSIS (The 4-Tier Matrix) Here's a matrix on competition analysis: Direct → same niche, same style Indirect → same niche, different format Adjacent → different niche, same audience (goldmine) Distant → different niche, same attention market (advanced)
